Privacy

Over & Out · overandoutgame.com · last updated 21 September 2026

Over & Out is a free cricket umpiring game. You can play it without signing in and without giving us anything. This page explains what happens if you choose to sign in to post scores to the leaderboard.

What we store if you sign in

  • Your Google account ID and email address — held by our sign-in provider (Supabase) so you can sign in again. Your email is never shown to other players.
  • A display name — taken from your Google name to start with. You can change it in the game. It appears on the leaderboard.
  • Your daily scores — the date, your score out of six, the six-square grid and the bowler. These appear on the leaderboard next to your display name.

That is the whole list. No location, no contacts, no tracking across other sites, no advertising, and we never sell or share your details with anyone.

What stays on your device

The game remembers today's over, your streak and your umpire rating in your browser. That stays on your phone or computer.

Anonymous counts

So we can see how many people play, the game adds a row to a counter when the page opens, when an over ends and when a share button is tapped. A row holds the date, the score, and whether the visit came through a shared link or from inside an app such as Instagram. It holds no name, no account, no IP address and no cookie.

When you share your over, the link carries a random six-character code that was made up in your browser. It lets us count how many new players arrive through shared links. It is not tied to your name or account, and it only travels in links you choose to send.

Removing your data

Email pattraynor92@gmail.com from the address you signed in with and we'll delete your account, name and scores.

Who runs this

Over & Out is a personal project run by Pat Traynor in the UK. Sign-in and the leaderboard database are provided by Supabase; Google sign-in is provided by Google under its own privacy policy.
